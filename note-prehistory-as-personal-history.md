# Note: prehistory as personal history

2026-10-02 — from Xyh, spoken, reconstructed; checks added by Claude Code.

## The idea as stated

- The Western Interior Seaway left marine fossils in the middle of North America
  and shorelines with organic matter. In the south that matter made soil fertile.
  Native Americans farmed it. Later, Europeans found it good for cotton, which ties
  it to Black slavery.
- So a prehistoric condition sets the stage for a personal story. Attach any
  Black American story to it (Xyh's example: *The Color Purple*) and the prehistory
  becomes part of that person's history.
- Xyh thinks this is what *Of Another Shore* is trying to do, and wonders whether
  it does so enough.
- The open question: the Philippines and Japan are archipelagos. What of
  Filipino-Japanese prehistory has cascaded into the present to make the condition
  Xyh is in?

## Checks (web search, 2026-10-02; not full-text)

- The link is real and documented. The Alabama Black Belt takes its name from dark
  soil weathered from Cretaceous chalk (Selma Group), the floor of the seaway's
  southern arm, and became a core cotton-plantation region after Native removal.
  Sources: [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/black-belt-us-history-cretaceous),
  [Encyclopedia of Alabama](https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/black-belt-region-in-alabama/),
  [Southern Spaces](https://southernspaces.org/2004/black-belt/).
- The soil comes from the Black Belt (Alabama, Mississippi), not the whole South.
  The chalk is marine sediment, so "organic matter at shorelines" is looser than
  the geology: it is calcium-rich seafloor deposits weathering into alkaline soil.
- *The Color Purple* is set in rural Georgia. Whether that setting sits on this
  geology has not been checked. Use a case that is located in the Black Belt, or
  check Georgia, before the example is relied on.
